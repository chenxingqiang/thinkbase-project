# Research Plan: 基于静态图的恶意软件分类方法研究

## Research Context
- Topic: 基于静态图的恶意软件分类方法研究
- Status: Initial Phase
- Fields: Computer Science, Data Science, Machine Learning

## Literature Review
### Key Findings
- **Finding 1:** Static-graph-based malware classification methods have shown significant effectiveness in identifying and categorizing various types of malware. These methods mainly rely on analyzing the structural properties and features of the static graphs generated from the binary files of malware
- **Finding 2:** Machine learning techniques, especially deep learning, have been widely used to improve the performance of static-graph-based malware classification. In particular, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have demonstrated promising results in this research field
- **Finding 3:** Feature extraction and selection play a critical role in static-graph-based malware classification. Many research studies have focused on developing novel techniques to extract and select the most discriminative features from static graphs for malware classification

### Research Gaps
- **Gap 1:** Despite the promising results, the robustness and stability of static-graph-based malware classification methods against anti-analysis techniques (e.g., obfuscation, packing) used by malware authors have not been thoroughly investigated
- **Gap 2:** The scalability of static-graph-based malware classification methods is still a significant concern. Most of the existing methods fail to efficiently handle large-scale malware datasets due to the high computational complexity of graph analysis and processing
- **Gap 3:** The interpretability of static-graph-based malware classification methods is another issue that has been overlooked by many researchers. It is challenging to understand and explain the decision-making process of these methods, especially those based on complex machine learning models like deep learning

### Future Directions
- **Direction 1:** Future research should focus on improving the robustness and stability of static-graph-based malware classification methods against various anti-analysis techniques. This could involve developing new techniques or adapting existing ones to detect and mitigate the effects of such techniques
- **Direction 2:** To address the scalability issue, future research could investigate the use of more efficient graph analysis and processing techniques. For instance, graph simplification and approximation methods could be explored to reduce the complexity of static graphs without significantly impacting the classification performance
- **Direction 3:** Efforts could also be made to enhance the interpretability of static-graph-based malware classification methods. This might involve incorporating explainability methods into the design of these methods or developing new methods that provide more interpretable classification results
